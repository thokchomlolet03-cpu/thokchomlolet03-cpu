# GRE preparation roadmap

[GRE home](README.md) · [Progress](PROGRESS.md)

Suggested 12-month plan from late 2026; adapt to shift work and baseline. Start with 45–60 minutes on five or six days a week, integrated with MIS mathematics rather than added twice.

| Phase | Weeks | Work | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic | 1 | Official practice test, untimed gap analysis, one essay | Baseline Q/V; essay review; error taxonomy |
| Foundations | 2–13 | Arithmetic, algebra, geometry, data; daily reading and vocabulary in context | 85% on fresh untimed topic sets |
| Reasoning | 14–26 | All verbal types and mixed quant; weekly issue essay | 85% fresh mixed untimed sets; clear essay argument |
| Exam practice | 27–39 | Timed sections, pacing, question selection, review weak topics | Consistent improvement on fresh timed sets |
| Simulation | 40–52 | Full tests, targeted repairs, writing feedback | Multiple comparable new timed practice scores near target |

Reserve at least one unused official full test for late-stage validation; do not interpret repeated-test scores as fresh evidence. Check admissions deadlines and test requirements before paying; aim for first attempt in late 2027 if ready, allowing retake time in 2028.

## Weekly pattern
- Three quant sessions: concept, independent problems, correction.
- Two verbal sessions: passage argument map, vocabulary in context, text completion/sentence equivalence.
- One essay or mixed timed section plus review.
- One rest/flex day. Short reading or spaced recall may be combined with commute where practical.

Daily session: 5 min recall, 15 min concept repair, 25 min independent practice, 10 min error review. Do not sacrifice sleep. Increase timed-test duration only when needed.

## First week
1. Take official baseline practice test under exam conditions (split only if unavoidable; label it).
2. Review every incorrect and guessed-correct answer; classify errors.
3. Repair weakest arithmetic/algebra topic using linked Khan exercises.
4. Read a challenging nonfiction passage; identify thesis, evidence, assumptions.
5. Practice verbal question types; learn 8–10 words in context.
6. Write a 30-minute issue essay; critique reasoning and clarity.
7. Review and select next week's weak topics.
