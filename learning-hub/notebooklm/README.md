## Topics and organization

### German language
- **German A1 / A2:** grammar, sentence structure, pronunciation, spaced practice, official course material.
- **German B1 / B2:** complex sentences, listening, authentic news, interview role-play and workplace writing.
- **German C1 / C2:** technical discussion, literature, nuances, advanced writing and exam-style critique.
- **German for IT:** troubleshooting conversations, job interviews, support documentation and infrastructure vocabulary.

### Systems and infrastructure
- **Linux and kernel:** processes, systemd, memory, filesystems, boot, observability.
- **Windows and endpoints:** PowerShell, AD DS, GPO, Entra, Intune, Jamf, macOS.
- **Networking and security:** TCP/IP, DNS, routing, BGP, TLS, certificates, VPNs.
- **Infrastructure and reliability:** virtualization, backup/restore, Terraform, Ansible, monitoring, outages and postmortems.
- **Certification preparation:** RHCSA, MD-102, CCNA and justified credential tracks.

### Quantum computing
- **Quantum fundamentals:** qubits, complex amplitudes, tensor products, measurement and entanglement.
- **IBM/Qiskit and Google/Cirq:** modern circuit APIs, compilation, noise, device connectivity and error correction.
- **Quantum + AI research:** trustworthy classical baselines, hybrid methods, resource accounting and scientific limitations. Start at the [separate quantum learning pathway](../quantum/README.md). Keep unproven claims labeled as hypotheses.

### Visual literacy, communication, and entrepreneurship
- **Design:** visual hierarchy, proportion, color theory, accessible typography and original comparisons.
- **Communication:** public-speaking practice and accurate explanations of work, with no confidential employer data.
- **Business:** marketing frameworks and anonymous, consent-based customer discovery. See the [human skills learning path](../personal-skills/README.md).

### Other independent research
- Keep science and exploratory-research notebooks separate from recruiter-facing employment evidence. Source-based summaries are not independent experiments or experimental proof.

## How to share a notebook safely

1. Open your notebook at [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/).
2. Before changing visibility, **audit the entire notebook's sources, generated content, uploaded documents, personal data and copyright permissions**.
3. If it is suitable for everyone to see and your account supports it, choose **Share → Anyone with a link**. Google states public notebooks can be accessed by others using a Google account; availability may depend on account and organization policy.
4. Copy the public share URL and check that it can be opened with a separate account without exposing anything you did not intend.
5. Use the [GitHub NotebookLM submission form](https://github.com/thokchomlolet03-cpu/thokchomlolet03-cpu/issues/new?template=notebooklm-link.yml). Provide title, category, German level if relevant, public notebook URL, original sources, and two or three original insights.
6. After review, add it to [INDEX.md](INDEX.md) and [CATALOG.csv](CATALOG.csv). **Posting an issue does not automatically update this index**. You can also ask me in chat to curate the supplied links when you have them.

**Critical privacy warning:** This GitHub repository and its issues are PUBLIC. Google cautions that even a *chat-view* share link does **not** fully prevent visitors from accessing the notebook's underlying sources and artifacts. Do **not** publish employer documents, corporate MDM/tenant records, credentials, patient/medical information, private family content or paid/copyright-restricted sources. If a notebook is private, keep the URL out of this public repository, or store a title-only, explicitly nonclickable private reference.

### No public share option?
If your account or organization restricts public sharing, don't bypass it. Either keep the notebook private and create an original public summary without private source content, or only publish in a legitimately private destination.

## How to make a deep dive worthwhile

For every notebook, use the [study note template](NOTE_TEMPLATE.md). Record:
- **First principles:** what mechanism/question are you investigating?
- **Sources:** publisher, date and reliability; does each source support the actual claim?
- **Contradictions:** identify at least one competing explanation and what observation would distinguish it.
- **Your original contribution:** a causal diagram, a correct German paragraph, a small lab experiment or a carefully reasoned synthesis.
- **Validation:** do not claim a result happened until you actually observed it or got meaningful feedback.

NotebookLM is excellent for navigating sources. It cannot replace personally completing a Linux lab, verifying an outage explanation, speaking German with another person or passing an official CEFR exam.
