# Human Behaviour, Society & Cooperation

[← Learning Hub](../README.md) · [Knowledge Atlas](../KNOWLEDGE_ATLAS.md) · [Learning philosophy](../LEARNING_PHILOSOPHY.md)

**Added 17 November 2026 · Independent study, not a credential claim.**

**Guiding question:** Why do people divide into groups, struggle to cooperate, and sometimes tolerate preventable suffering—and what evidence-based actions help? Human dignity, curiosity, and practical benefit are the motivation. This is an optional cross-disciplinary learning library, not a mandatory course queue or a career requirement.

## 5. Read original research and its limitations

- [Pettigrew and Tropp (2006), A meta-analytic test of intergroup contact theory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737372/) — freely readable abstract; foundational evidence, not a universal prescription.
- [Paluck, Green and Green (2019), The contact hypothesis re-evaluated](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/contact-hypothesis-reevaluated/142C913E7FA9E121277B29E994124EC5) — open-access critical review; examine causal evidence and generalizability.
- [OpenLearn — Online versus face-to-face intergroup contact](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/course/view.php?id=17468) — short research-informed resource.

Read both supportive and critical evidence. Contact is not guaranteed to reduce prejudice under every condition; unequal power, discrimination, incentives, institutional rules and safety deserve attention. Do not use theories to assign motives or characteristics to individuals based on nationality, ethnicity or religion.

## 6. An optional eight-week starting path

| Week | Focus | First action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Social psychology | OpenLearn Making Sense of Ourselves, prejudice section |
| 2 | Evidence on prejudice | OpenStax chapter 12; compare both intergroup contact papers |
| 3 | Biology and culture | Stanford introductory lectures plus an OpenStax anthropology chapter |
| 4 | Cooperation | Yale Game Theory introductory lectures and CORE social dilemmas |
| 5 | Poverty and hunger | MIT Challenge of World Poverty, nutrition and education topics |
| 6 | Conflict | MIT International Relations and Kaya Conflict Sensitivity |
| 7 | Humanitarian standards | Sphere in Practice and IFRC learning platform |
| 8 | Evidence and application | J-PAL Evaluating Social Programs; identify one modest, safe, community-led action |

No need to finish all courses before acting. Begin with one resource, write one question and one observation, and remain willing to revise your beliefs. All resources are uncompleted unless independently recorded otherwise.

## 7. Turn understanding into responsible action

- Practice listening across cultural boundaries, with respect and consent. Avoid collecting identifiable information, secretly recording people, treating coworkers as research subjects, or posting workplace experiences publicly.
- Distinguish observed behaviour from explanations; consider language barriers, stress, incentives, unequal power and discrimination rather than assuming cultural or biological causes.
- Ask what a community actually needs before proposing a technological intervention. Compare alternatives, include local voices and measure outcomes, including possible harms.
- For volunteering, use legitimate organizations and follow their training, safeguarding, security, legal and professional scope requirements. Do not enter active conflict zones independently or provide clinical care without appropriate qualifications.
- Keep private finances, donations, personal conversations and employer information out of this public repository.
