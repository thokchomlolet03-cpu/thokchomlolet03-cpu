# From technical projects to real customers — an execution lab

[← Single active Learning Hub](../README.md) · [Curated free sources](../WORLD_CLASS_FREE_COURSES.md) · [Human skills](../personal-skills/README.md)

**Added 16 November 2026. Status: planned; no customers, sales, revenue, product-market fit or venture-scale outcomes are claimed here.** This track answers a gap in the existing learning system: sophisticated learning and prototyping are not substitutes for distribution, willingness to pay, pricing discipline and actual customer retention. This is an **optional part-time entrepreneurial experiment**, not another mandatory career curriculum or a guarantee of unusual wealth.

## The one question to answer

**Can I solve a painful, recurring problem for a specific group of people, reach them reliably, and get them to pay more than the cost of serving them?**

Do not optimize for fundraising, press, a complicated architecture or the next certificate until you have credible evidence on that question. Keep the salary, employability study and household financial resilience as the primary protection against downside.

## Ninety-day evidence experiment — time-boxed, not a promised result

Pick **one** testable product problem and **one** target customer group. Mangal Visual (scientifically accurate explainer videos) is a candidate, **not a validated business**; other candidate problems may prove more urgent.

| Stage | Activities that create evidence | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Days 1–30 — Discover** | Write a one-sentence problem and target buyer; make a list of reachable prospects; ask for ~10–15 interviews, record actual responses (consensually, privately), inspect existing solutions and budget/time costs | Are there repeated, specific problems and evidence of a budget or pressing workaround? If not, revise segment/problem before building |
| **Days 31–60 — Deliver** | Offer a small **manual or prototype-supported** result to a few fitting users; measure time to deliver, correctness, quality, repeat requests and objections; request a small paid pilot if lawful and appropriate | Does at least one user accept a meaningful commitment (paid when feasible)? If not, learn whether value, trust, price or distribution is missing |
| **Days 61–90 — Test repeatability** | Deliver pilots, ask for a second use/referral, document true delivery and support costs, compare prices and channels, and choose continue / pivot / stop | Is there observable repeat demand or a viable test to pursue next? Do not invent revenue or equate interview compliments with sales |

**Numbers above are planning prompts, not proof thresholds, success guarantees or orders to solicit people without consent.** An answer of “no” is valuable evidence, not failure. If time is scarce, make the experiment longer rather than sacrificing German, systems career work or income.

### Operating budget and boundaries

- Keep the current job while validating; never assume a UAE employment visa permits independent commercial activity. Review employment terms, applicable business activity permits and payment rules *before* paid pilots.
- Do not commit household emergency funds, take loans, pay for startup formation, subscribe to expensive SaaS, or buy ads simply to complete this plan.
- No sale is recorded until actual confirmed money is received or a properly documented binding order exists; record expressions of interest separately.
- Protect customer data, school/medical data and unpublished content. Anonymize public learning notes; keep consented contact details private.
- For an education product, evaluate **scientific accuracy, intellectual property rights and honest source attribution** as part of product value—not just animation quality.

## Only four metrics initially

1. **Problem evidence:** count distinct interviewed prospective buyers, their actual current workaround, urgency and whether they have spending authority. Do not count friends' encouragement as demand.
2. **Conversion:** number of targeted, respectful contacts → discovery calls → real trials → confirmed paid commitments. Show numerators/denominators and small-sample uncertainty.
3. **Value delivery:** minutes/hours to deliver an accurate output, corrections requested, follow-up usage and customer willingness to repeat.
4. **Economics:** price actually paid, processing/compute/service costs, contribution margin and acquisition effort; calculate CAC/LTV only with meaningful data, not hypothetical “3:1” headlines.

**Suggested 2-hour weekly loop:** 20 minutes read a relevant passage → 50 minutes speak to users or reach prospects → 40 minutes deliver or improve the smallest useful result → 10 minutes record evidence and choose the next test. More reading is permitted only if it resolves a specific blocker.

**Public GitHub is not a CRM.** Do not publish prospect names, emails, chat logs, interview recordings, confidential business/medical/student data, payment details, or identifiable data without express authority. Keep a private contact list if legally appropriate; the repository should contain only aggregated or synthetic examples and your own explanatory work.

## After first demand — not before

Once genuine paying and repeat use is observed, revisit distribution economics, retention, hiring, financing, equity and legal structure. The [Stripe founder equity guide](https://stripe.com/guides/atlas/equity) is background; consult the relevant professional in the correct jurisdiction. **A billion-dollar ambition is not an evidence milestone.** Sustainable, repeatable customer value is.
