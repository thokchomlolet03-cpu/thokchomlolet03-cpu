# Marketing, customer discovery and future entrepreneurship

[← Human skills](README.md) · [Practice templates](PRACTICE_TEMPLATES.md)

**Goal:** learn why people adopt products and services, how to describe value and how to test whether a real problem is worth solving—before spending substantial resources building a company.

**Do not confuse marketing with persuasive exaggeration.** The first task is finding real customer problems and communicating a truthful solution, not creating flashy claims or collecting followers.

## Two separate uses of marketing

**Professional positioning:** help potential employers/collaborators quickly understand the problem class you work on and **what you personally verified**. The [recruiter portfolio](../../systems-infrastructure/RECRUITER_PORTFOLIO.md) remains the source of evidence.

**Business positioning:** describe a buyer's actual task, existing workaround, cost or frustration, and a verifiable improvement your solution may provide.

## Customer discovery: five conversations

Choose a **small, legal, low-risk problem** with potential users. Interview five people *with their consent*, outside any employer-confidential workflow.

Ask for actual past events, for example:
- "Tell me about the last time you encountered this problem."
- "What did you do to solve it?"
- "Which part consumed the most time or money?"
- "What alternatives have you tried?"
- "What happened when it was not solved?"

Avoid "Would you definitely buy my AI startup?" or leading them toward the answer you want. Five interviews are an **exploratory sample, not statistical proof of market demand**.

Create an **anonymized pattern summary**: common problem, frequency *as reported*, present alternatives, unresolved questions, and a tiny ethical prototype test. Don't publish names, direct quotes, personal contacts or private employer information without permission.

## The one-page value proposition

- **Customer:** which specific group?
- **Situation:** what concrete job are they trying to accomplish?
- **Pain or constraint:** what demonstrable cost, uncertainty or obstacle?
- **Alternative:** what do they currently do?
- **Proposed improvement:** which observable metric would improve?
- **Claim boundary:** what is still speculative, unmeasured or dependent on external approval?
- **Next test:** one small prototype or customer observation with a stop condition.

## Business risk discipline

Treat future commercial experiments as separate from your current income and immigration obligations. Start with free interviews, public market research and low-cost prototypes. Avoid paid advertising, legal incorporation or recurring subscriptions until you have evidence that a target problem matters to users and a credible model for delivering value.

**Exit criterion:** write a one-page value proposition grounded in five ethical conversations and explicitly state what the evidence does **not** yet demonstrate. Revenue and customer counts should not be presented unless observed and verifiable.
