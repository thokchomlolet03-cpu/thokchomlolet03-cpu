# Professional communication — visibility without pretending to be extroverted

[← Human skills](README.md) · [Practice templates](PRACTICE_TEMPLATES.md)

**Goal:** make real contributions understandable and findable, ask for opportunities and build trust. Effective professional visibility is not the same as speaking frequently or claiming work others did.

## Four capabilities to practice

### C01 — Describe your contribution accurately

Use **Situation → Action → Observed Result → What I'd improve**. If metrics were not measured, do not invent a percentage. Example pattern:

"Users reported [problem]. I investigated [method and scope]. The result was [actual observed outcome]. Next I would verify [remaining uncertainty]."

A private accomplishment log can support job interviews and performance discussions. Do not put employer-internal incidents or identifying details into public GitHub.

### C02 — Speak once when it matters

Prepare a brief relevant question or observation before each team meeting. Speaking more is not the goal; speaking **more clearly and intentionally** is.

### C03 — Ask for opportunities

Frame an explicit request: what role or skill you are interested in, what evidence you already have and what trial responsibility might be appropriate. Seek feedback from supervisors about the gap without assuming a promotion is guaranteed.

### C04 — Present a technical artifact

Choose one GitHub lab. Explain: problem, architecture, what you tested, actual result, limits, and next experiment. Record two versions—**2 minutes** for nontechnical listeners and **5 minutes** for technical interviewers. Avoid reading slides word-for-word.

## A low-pressure progression

- Week 1: send one well-structured written update.
- Week 2: make one short contribution in a discussion.
- Week 3: deliver a recorded two-minute explanation and self-review.
- Week 4: request feedback or attend a Toastmasters guest meeting.

Progress is demonstrated by clear communication and useful response, **not** audience size, vocal volume, popularity or guaranteed career outcomes.
