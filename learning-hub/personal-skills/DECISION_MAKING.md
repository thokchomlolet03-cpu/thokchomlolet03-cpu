# Decision-making under uncertainty — protect essentials, experiment safely

[← Human skills](README.md) · [Practice templates](PRACTICE_TEMPLATES.md)

**Goal:** make deliberate decisions even when there is uncertainty, without treating every fear as irrational or every leap as courageous.

## Foundation reading: free public-health workbooks

| Sequence | Source | Focus | Useful modules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [CCI — Put Off Procrastinating](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Procrastination) | Recognize postponement, excuses and short-term relief | Modules 1–3 and 6 (tolerating discomfort) |
| 2 | [CCI — What? Me Worry?](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Worry-and-Rumination) | Distinguish problem-solving from repetitive worry; uncertainty | Module 7 (problem-solving), 9 (accepting uncertainty) |
| 3 | [Stanford d.school — Design Thinking Bootleg](https://dschool.stanford.edu/tools/design-thinking-bootleg) | Empathize → define → ideate → prototype → test | Use one rapid prototype card |
| 4 | [Y Combinator Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/) | Validate a business idea through small tests before major investment | Only after completing the low-risk exercises |

The CCI material is educational and **not a diagnosis**. Individual experience, mental health and risk tolerance vary. If worry disrupts daily life significantly, a qualified professional may be more appropriate than self-study alone.

## One decision filter

Before acting or avoiding, write down:
1. **Reality:** what is actually known, and what is only a prediction?
2. **Worst plausible loss:** money, time, reputation, permission, relationship, or safety.
3. **Reversibility:** is this a small experiment or a long-term commitment?
4. **Protection:** what financial, legal, privacy and employment boundaries are non-negotiable?
5. **Learning value:** what useful information will be gained even if the attempt fails?
6. **Feedback date:** when will you assess actual results instead of repeatedly imagining outcomes?

**Example:** attend an introductory professional event (reversible, small cost) rather than leave your job for an untested startup (high irreversible cost). The point is **selective courage**, not indiscriminate risk taking.

## Mini-experiment: prediction vs observation

For one safe action each week:
- **Prediction:** "If I ask one clear question in a meeting, what do I expect?"
- **Budget:** 10–30 minutes, no purchase and no disclosure of confidential information.
- **Action:** try it in an appropriate setting.
- **Evidence:** record what happened without judging yourself based on one reaction.
- **Revision:** what was accurate, what was not, and which small test is next?

Keep sensitive predictions and personal details in a private journal, **not this public repository**. Only publish anonymized methods or deliberately chosen, safe learning outcomes.

## Avoid two opposite errors

- **Avoidance:** avoiding harmless, reversible opportunities solely to eliminate discomfort.
- **Recklessness:** ignoring serious economic or legal risks in pursuit of a dramatic story about bravery.

**Exit criterion:** make three reversible, well-bounded experiments; record what each taught you; identify a condition under which you would *not* take the risk.
