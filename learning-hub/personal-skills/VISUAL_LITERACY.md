# Visual literacy — design principles, color, clothing, spaces

[← Human skills](README.md) · [Practice templates](PRACTICE_TEMPLATES.md) · [12-week plan](ROADMAP_12_WEEKS.md)

**Goal:** learn to *explain* why a composition looks coherent or confusing; then test your choices in different settings. Beauty is partly subjective and cultural. Effective visual communication also has assessable qualities such as readability, contrast, spacing and hierarchy.

## Clothing and personal proportions

Learn **fit and relationships**, not rigid rules for a body type. Explore shoulder fit, trouser rise, sleeve length, jacket length, clothing volume and visual continuity. A garment can be well made yet fit one wearer poorly; tailoring often matters more than a fashion label.

**Controlled photo exercise:** use 3 existing outfits, the same camera height, distance and lighting. Change only one variable each round (trouser break, top length, waist position or color). Write your own judgment before getting feedback. Evaluate comfort and movement, not only photographs. Do not publish identifying photos to GitHub without explicitly deciding to do so.

**Color near the face:** compare existing fabrics in indirect daylight and against a neutral background. Evaluate lighting conditions and what *you* prefer; online seasonal-color systems and fixed skin-tone rules are not universal science. Start with neutral colors you already own, add one accent, and avoid costly shopping based on an unverified palette.

Optional editorial style reading: [GQ Style](https://www.gq.com/style) (not an individualized fitting service).

## Buildings, interiors and vehicles

- **Building:** context, light, material, scale, roof, landscaping and maintenance matter. Look at paint samples in several daylight conditions before making a large purchase. [Benjamin Moore exterior color ideas](https://www.benjaminmoore.com/en-us/project-ideas-inspiration/exteriors).
- **Interior:** test a warm neutral, cool neutral and one accent; compare across morning/day/night. Consider material textures and lived-in function.
- **Vehicle:** evaluate color in different lighting and practical heat/visibility/maintenance trade-offs, not only a brochure rendering.
- **Icons:** select an icon grid and one consistent visual vocabulary; check both 24px and large versions.
- **Fonts:** limit variation, check legibility on mobile and low-vision contrast. Don't choose based solely on aesthetic appeal.

## Three practical projects

**V01 — Visual analysis atlas.** Ten screenshots/photos of designs *you are authorized to reuse* or links to originals. For each, note visual hierarchy, balance, color, what does not work, alternative, and feedback. External copyrighted images should usually be linked, not copied into GitHub.

**V02 — Personal wardrobe experiment.** Rebuild five outfits from existing clothes, with a controlled change and a written explanation. Store photos privately. Outcome is better justified choices, not a universal attractiveness score.

**V03 — Redesign a GitHub/project graphic.** Make two icon/typography/color versions, test thumbnail legibility, get feedback from two people, and keep a brief revision log. The before/after comparison is a genuine portfolio artifact if created by you and permissions are clear.

## Exit criterion

Explain at least five visual choices, reproduce one design improvement, and distinguish **your preference** from functional accessibility requirements and from other people's preferences. This is a practiced skill, not a certification.
