# The Knowledge Atlas — a lifelong library across disciplines

[← Learning Hub](README.md) · [World-class course directory](WORLD_CLASS_FREE_COURSES.md) · [University studies](university/README.md) · [Mathematics](mathematics/README.md) · [Human skills](personal-skills/README.md)

**Added 17 November 2026.** This is a **library, not a syllabus, career funnel, mandatory queue, ranked list or productivity test.** It exists to encounter ideas *outside* computing and familiar expertise, including fields whose usefulness is not yet obvious. A field belongs here because it can expand understanding, not because it is currently employable.

### What counts as world-class here?

A collection may qualify because it is made by (1) the university or research center advancing the subject; (2) an actual government/scientific institution curating original evidence; (3) a leading disciplinary scholar or cultural museum; or (4) the organization that builds an important technology. **Free access to substantial material is required; a free credential is not.** Distinguish full *courses* with exercises from free lectures, museums, reference libraries, tools and open datasets. A high-status logo never replaces critical reading, problems, independent evidence or real-world testing.

**Access key:** **Course** = structured free lessons; **Open lectures** = full university lectures, often without assessments; **Interactive** = free experiments/simulations; **Primary sources** = original documents and research data; **Reference** = high-quality material, not a course. Optional paid certificates, books or services may exist on the same provider site. Provider inventories and policies can change.

> Browse by **question**, not by existing identity: “How did societies change?”, “Why do brains remember?”, “How does food reach people?”, “Can we measure an ecosystem?”, “Why does a building feel beautiful?”, “What is a good scientific explanation?”

## K. Explore an unexpected connection (optional prompts)

- **Network resilience ↔ ecology:** what can food webs, fault tolerance and cascading outages teach each other?
- **Scientific uncertainty ↔ financial valuation:** when is a precise number falsely reassuring?
- **Architecture ↔ computing interfaces:** how do constraints shape human behavior?
- **Epidemiology ↔ information spread:** what makes a mechanism contagious across different kinds of networks?
- **Language learning ↔ memory science:** what does cognitive evidence reveal about passive exposure and deliberate recall?
- **History ↔ startup narratives:** how many successful innovations looked inevitable only in hindsight?
- **Art history ↔ data visualization:** which choices quietly change what a viewer believes?

Do not assume an analogy is evidence; formulate a test, find a counterexample and record uncertainty.

## How to use this library without shrinking it

1. **Browse freely.** You never need a career reason to open a subject.
2. **Sample before committing.** One lecture, problem, dataset or document can tell you whether to explore further.
3. **Go deep when a real question grabs you.** Doing a complete course is an option, not a condition of belonging in the library.
4. **Preserve differences in evidence.** A museum exhibit is not a peer-reviewed paper; a lecture is not a completed certificate; a simulation is not a real-world experiment.
5. **Leave room for surprises.** If a subject is absent, add a high-quality source and a genuine question. This directory is intentionally incomplete and expandable.

For structured study in familiar areas, continue to use [the World-Class Free Learning Collection](WORLD_CLASS_FREE_COURSES.md), [the original 31-entry university course catalog](university/COURSE_CATALOG.csv), [human-skills catalog](personal-skills/RESOURCE_CATALOG.csv) and the subject-specific sublibraries. The Knowledge Atlas is a separate entrypoint for intellectual breadth.

**Verification note (17 November 2026):** The provider-owned landing pages were selected for free material. Some links lead to broad catalogs containing a mix of free and paid options, and some resources are historical archives. Individual course enrollment, certificate availability, geographical restrictions and exact prices should be rechecked at the source.
