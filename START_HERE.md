## Why this exists

I previously worked in enterprise Level 2 IT support, and I am building toward deeper systems and infrastructure engineering: understanding hardware, operating systems, networks, identity, automation, and failures from first principles. I also pursue independent software and scientific research questions, and I write about ideas that interest me beyond technology.

This is a **living workbench**, not a finished certificate wall or an attempt to portray planned work as completed work. I study for **breadth and depth without an artificial deadline**: engineering foundations, critical reasoning, languages, visual judgment, communication, market understanding, real engineering case studies and meeting people. [Read the learning philosophy](learning-hub/LEARNING_PHILOSOPHY.md). Publishing a plan makes the next experiment explicit; publishing actual outputs, tests, mistakes and limitations makes the result reviewable. My working cycle is:

**Ask a question → study original sources → predict what should happen → build/test in an authorized lab → capture observations → challenge the explanation → revise and publish.**

The learning and writing branches are intentionally separate from employer-facing proof of technical competency.

## How this collection works

### 1. The public profile is the front door

[README.md](README.md) provides a short professional introduction, selected projects, and navigation. It is not intended to list every course, every essay, or every learning experiment.

### 2. Systems engineering is the technical workbench

The [systems-infrastructure directory](systems-infrastructure/README.md) contains a 24-month learning plan, certification decisions, code, incident exercises and project acceptance criteria. The [evidence policy](systems-infrastructure/EVIDENCE_POLICY.md) and [QA report](systems-infrastructure/QUALITY_ASSURANCE.md) distinguish authored source code, automated test results, owner-executed labs and professional experience.

**Please distinguish these statuses:**
- **Planned:** an idea or specification, not a functioning project.
- **Source authored / tests run:** inspectable code and scoped checks; not necessarily tested by me on my own machine.
- **Demonstrated in my own lab:** reproducible environment, actual observations, failures and recovery evidence documented by me.
- **Professional experience:** real work described accurately and within confidentiality boundaries.

A green GitHub Actions check means the configured code tests passed. It does not prove that I personally completed a Windows Server lab or operated an enterprise fleet.

### 3. The Learning Hub is a practical bookshelf

The [Learning Hub](learning-hub/README.md) reduces browsing friction. It links directly to external courses for [German from beginner to advanced](learning-hub/german/README.md), engineering postmortems from real companies, and a directory for source-based [NotebookLM deep dives](learning-hub/notebooklm/INDEX.md). Those sources belong to their respective publishers.

**Reading something is not the same as understanding or validating it.** I try to turn useful questions into independent practice and testable explanations.

### 4. Human skills are a separate practical development track

The [human skills learning track](learning-hub/personal-skills/README.md) offers one-click visual-design and personal style resources, structured decision-making, professional communication, and marketing/customer discovery. It includes an optional [12-week sequence](learning-hub/personal-skills/ROADMAP_12_WEEKS.md) and explicitly empty progress log. This is **learning and experimentation**, not professional design/marketing experience or an earned certificate. Personal journal details stay private.

### 5. Quantum computing is a separate, long-horizon research track

The [quantum computing roadmap](learning-hub/quantum/README.md) is intended for first-principles study and future scientific-computing research—not a claim that quantum technology presently accelerates AI models. It connects [IBM's Qiskit](learning-hub/quantum/QISKIT.md), [Google Cirq](learning-hub/quantum/GOOGLE_CIRQ.md), error/noise models, and [honest quantum–classical comparisons](learning-hub/quantum/QUANTUM_AI_RESEARCH.md). Current infrastructure career evidence stays separate.

### 6. Essays & Ideas is a separate public notebook

[Essays & Ideas](essays/README.md) links to published pieces wherever they live—Medium, Substack or another platform in the future. Essays may include open questions, opinions, technical reflections and creative stories. A narrative voice is not automatically autobiographical, and an essay describing a technical ambition is not a verified engineering accomplishment.

The index records original links, categories and publication dates; future essays can be added without rebuilding the profile.

### 7. Larger projects have their own repositories

The [Project Guide](PROJECT_GUIDE.md) points to independent software and research repositories, including [Terraform Mastery](https://github.com/thokchomlolet03-cpu/terraform-mastery) and [Discovery Intelligence](https://github.com/thokchomlolet03-cpu/discovery_intelligence_system). Each project's own README, tests, evidence and constraints are its source of truth. Independent research ideas and hypothetical outcomes are not presented as proven scientific results.

## Principles and boundaries

- **Evidence before claims.** A course, generated explanation, working prototype, production experience and earned certification are not interchangeable.
- **Failures are useful data.** Good projects include what didn't work, not just the polished result.
- **Source and opinion must stay distinguishable.** I link original papers, vendor documentation and incident reports rather than copying them wholesale.
- **Public by design, private by default.** No employer/customer artifacts, personal secrets, sensitive notebook source material or unauthorized lab captures.
- **Open to changing my mind.** Plans, arguments and technical conclusions may be corrected as stronger evidence appears.
